Methodology v1.2

Define the opportunity before looking at the quarterback.

The opportunity rule, viability gate and downstream models are applied consistently across the historical dataset. Named quarterbacks and realized outcomes are not used to tune the viability threshold.

Qualifying state

The possession must begin in the fourth quarter or overtime with the offense tied or trailing by one through eight points.

Literal final first

Selection starts with the team's literal final meaningful offensive possession. A later nonqualifying meaningful possession blocks an earlier qualifying one.

Viability gate

A qualifying possession must have at least a 5.0% pre-drive probability of reaching the applicable scoring-threat zone, unless it already begins inside that zone. Only a qualifying possession that fails this gate may be skipped backward.

Conversion

Tied: take the lead. Down 1–6: tie or take the lead. Down 7: offensive touchdown converts independent of the PAT. Down 8: the offense must tie or lead, normally via touchdown plus two-point conversion.

Kicker treatment

A successful tying/go-ahead field goal converts. Missed or blocked attempts of 45 yards or less are treated as offensive conversions; 46–55 yards are kicker no-decisions; 56+ yards go to manual review.

QB attribution

The materially participating quarterback is credited. Possessions with multiple material QB participants are manually reviewed rather than guessed.

Why treat the terminal opportunity differently?

In the preserved literal-final diagnostic sample, the fourth-down go rate is 56.9% on the terminal opportunity versus 17.7% on earlier qualifying fourth-quarter possessions. The terminal punt rate is 17.7% versus 61.2% earlier. These diagnostics support treating the terminal possession as a distinct decision regime.

xCDCR and CAE

xCDCR is a cross-fitted expected conversion probability from the selected drive's starting situation using era, score state, time remaining and field position. CAE is observed CDCR minus xCDCR in percentage points. Player-level CAE is reported with uncertainty.

Standardized CDCR (sCDCR)

sCDCR estimates a partially pooled quarterback effect on top of xCDCR and evaluates quarterbacks over a common reference opportunity distribution. Its rank-stability checks pass across alternate specifications.

The held-out predictive checks are mixed, with no clear predictive gain. The study therefore treats sCDCR as a descriptive standardization/shrinkage estimate with uncertainty, not as a demonstrated predictive improvement over calibrated xCDCR.

Clutch Response Events

Every successful qualifying late-game response is preserved as a descriptive event. Response Events do not create a second success percentage because retaining earlier successes while superseding earlier failures would create an outcome-dependent denominator.

Study coverage

1999 through the completion of Week 3 of the 2026 season. Source coverage is tracked independently from the date of the latest game that actually produced a selected CDCR opportunity.
