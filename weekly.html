2026 weekly journal

Five weeks of pressure: who delivered, who missed, and what CFR saw.

A week-by-week look at the 2026 season through Week 5 using the locked CFR methodology. This is an editorial reading of the published decision ledger—not a new metric and not a replacement for the main research article.

59

Resolved quarterback clutch opportunities through Week 5.

24

Conversions, a 40.7% observed rate.

42.8%

Average xCDCR expectation across those opportunities.

Week 1 — Immediate proof that the scoreboard is not enough

Week 1 produced 4 conversions in 8 resolved opportunities (50.0%), slightly above the 46.7% average xCDCR expectation. The most striking result belonged to Tyler Shough. New Orleans began an overtime possession down seven with only a 13.0% expected conversion rate, yet the offense produced the touchdown required for a CFR conversion. The Saints still lost, which is exactly why CFR separates the quarterback's final offensive opportunity from the eventual team result.

Josh Allen also converted an average-difficulty opportunity against Houston, starting at Buffalo's own 4 while down one. At the other end of the week, Drake Maye failed a very favorable 70.6% opportunity against Seattle with an interception. Week 1's lesson was immediate: a conversion can occur in a loss, and a failure can be especially meaningful when the situation strongly favored success.

Week 2 — The quarterbacks beat expectation

Week 2 was the strongest collective week of the first five: 7 conversions in 12 resolved opportunities (58.3%) against a 52.9% expected rate. Daniel Jones converted an average-difficulty overtime opportunity against Kansas City with only a 40.0% xCDCR, even though Indianapolis ultimately lost. Jalen Hurts converted a 44.3% opportunity against Tennessee and Philadelphia won.

Geno Smith's overtime possession against Green Bay stands out in the opposite direction. The Jets began tied with a 63.7% expected conversion rate and the possession ended in a punt. Baker Mayfield also failed an essentially coin-flip opportunity against Cleveland. Trevor Lawrence's Week 2 failure against Denver came in a harder 34.9% situation, so CFR treats it as a miss without equating it to the more favorable opportunities that went unconverted.

Week 3 — Several genuine clutch conversions, and one costly miss

Week 3 produced 6 conversions in 14 resolved opportunities (42.9%), modestly above the 39.8% expectation. Lamar Jackson converted a 43.3% late opportunity against Dallas with only seven seconds showing at the start state recorded by CFR. Dak Prescott had already converted a 44.3% opportunity in the same game, despite Dallas ultimately losing. Daniel Jones also converted a 44.3% opportunity against Houston.

The week's clearest shortfall relative to expectation was Joe Burrow against Pittsburgh. Cincinnati's final qualifying opportunity carried a 62.9% xCDCR—favorable by CFR standards—but ended with a lost fumble. That is more informative than simply labeling every late turnover equally. Matthew Stafford's interception against Denver, for example, came from a situation with only a 9.6% expected conversion rate; the result was a failure, but the starting conditions were dramatically worse.

Week 4 — The first real clutch drought

Week 4 was the roughest quarterback clutch week of the opening stretch: only 3 conversions in 14 resolved opportunities (21.4%), well below the 35.0% average expectation. Dak Prescott, Matthew Stafford and Deshaun Watson accounted for the three conversions, all from favorable situations.

The more revealing story was how many reasonable chances went unused. Josh Allen failed a 44.3% opportunity against New England with an interception. Tampa Bay's Jalon Daniels failed a 48.2% opportunity against Green Bay on downs. Jacoby Brissett failed a 38.2% opportunity against the Giants with an interception. Week 4 is the clearest example so far of an entire slate collectively coming up short of what the situations suggested was achievable.

Week 5 — Hurts delivers; Daniels leaves the biggest opportunity unused

Week 5 contained 11 resolved quarterback opportunities and 4 conversions (36.4%), below the 42.8% xCDCR expectation. Jalen Hurts produced the week's strongest conversion relative to starting difficulty: Philadelphia began down two with 1:35 remaining at its own 38, a 44.3% xCDCR situation, and converted the opportunity. Philadelphia still lost, again reinforcing the distinction between quarterback execution and final team outcome.

Jayden Daniels had the largest Week 5 shortfall relative to expectation. Washington began an overtime possession tied with a 63.7% xCDCR and failed to take the lead. Jameis Winston also failed an approximately even 49.1% overtime opportunity with a lost fumble. Josh Allen and Tyler Huntley both converted favorable 63.7% overtime opportunities, while Brock Purdy converted an 86.3% very favorable situation—successful execution, but one that CFR already considered highly likely.

A Week 5 data correction worth documenting

Jacksonville's final sequence against Philadelphia originally exposed a quarterback-attribution edge case. A kickoff return placed Jacksonville in field-goal range and the winning kick followed without Trevor Lawrence taking an offensive snap. CFR now preserves that terminal team possession internally for auditability but excludes it from the public quarterback decision ledger. Lawrence receives neither a conversion nor an unresolved QB row for that special-teams-only sequence.

What the first five weeks suggest

Through Week 5, quarterbacks converted 24 of 59 resolved CFR opportunities (40.7%) against an aggregate expected rate of 42.8%. That gap is small enough that the broader story is not that quarterbacks are collectively over- or under-performing. The value is in the individual situations: who converted when the odds were modest, who failed when the odds were favorable, and which apparent late-game failures were actually low-probability desperation situations from the start.

That is the purpose of this weekly journal. The main CFR study remains the methodological foundation; this page turns each week's new evidence into a readable football story without changing the underlying rules.
